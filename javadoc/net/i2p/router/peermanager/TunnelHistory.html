      • DECAY_INTERVAL_MS

        static final long DECAY_INTERVAL_MS
        How often the lifetime counters decay, as a whole interval.

        Eight hours, not fifteen minutes. At 3/4 per interval the shorter period made a peer forget its own record faster than it could accumulate one: 50 accepted builds fell to 16 within an hour and under 1 within four, so a *good* history decayed exactly as fast as a bad one. Eight hours gives a half-life near 19 hours, so an established record takes about 128 hours to fall to 1% and a peer's last few days of behaviour dominate its ratio.

      • decayLifetimeCounters

        public void decayLifetimeCounters(long now)
        Apply the lifetime-counter decay if its interval has elapsed.

        Deliberately does not coalesce the RateStats. Those are folded by coalesceStats() on the caller's own cadence, and folding them again here would advance their windows twice as often as the statistic's consumers expect. A caller that only wants the decay — the tier reorganise walking every candidate — can call this cheaply: one volatile read and one comparison, with real work only on the pass that actually crosses the interval.

        Must be called with the write lock held when called from the reorganise, so the tier membership being read and the counters being decayed are consistent.

        Parameters:
        now - current time in ms

      • coalesceStats

        public void coalesceStats()
        Coalesce the rate statistics and periodically decay lifetime counters.

        Lifetime counters (_lifetimeAgreedTo, _lifetimeRejected, _lifetimeTimedOut, _lifetimeFailed) are decayed at 75% per 8 hours (matching DBHistory). This prevents unbounded growth across restarts and ensures stale history does not permanently block peers from tiers. Recency is handled by the RateStats (10-minute and 1-hour windows) and by selection gates.
